The Problem

Companies buy ocean carbon credits to offset emissions, trusting sellers' claims about how much CO₂ a kelp farm or mangrove restoration project captured. There's no independent, ocean-specific way to check those claims against real satellite data — buyers are paying for promises, not proof. Established carbon-market auditors (Pachama, Sylvera, NCX) focus almost entirely on forests; ocean ecosystems are an open gap.

What this is Blue Carbon Verification

Ocean carbon credits (from kelp forests, mangroves, and seagrass meadows) are sold based on claimed carbon capture — but almost no one independently verifies whether the growth actually happened. This tool simulates pulling satellite imagery over a project site, measuring canopy area change over a 12-month period, and converting that into an estimated CO2 sequestration figure — then compares it against what the project developer claims they captured, flagging mismatches.

Why Now

Regulation is forcing the issue: corporate buyers face growing pressure to prove offsets are real, not greenwashed. The underlying detection method here isn't invented for a pitch — a peer-reviewed 2020 remote sensing study mapped giant kelp forests globally using this exact approach (Sentinel-2 spectral indices) with 88%+ accuracy. That means this can grow from prototype into a working tool on real, free satellite data (Sentinel-2 + Google Earth Engine), not just a simulation.
